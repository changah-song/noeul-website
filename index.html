Features

A reading lamp, not a casino.

Everything here serves one idea: the more you read, the more Korean you know. The tools stay out of the way.

Tap any word

Instant definitions, romanization or pinyin, and the hanja behind Korean words — tap any single character for its own meaning. AI explains what a word means here, in this sentence.

Words under words

Short translations sit under harder words and pinyin above Chinese ones. Underlines are tuned to your level, and dissolve into pixels once you've read past them.

Listen mode

Your phone reads the book aloud, sentence by sentence — with the screen locked, or from the lock-screen player. Hear translations, pause on underlined words, and set the speed.

Bring your own books — and songs

Public-domain Korean classics are built in — 감자, 날개, 운수 좋은 날. Import any Korean or Chinese EPUB or PDF, or search for a song by title, artist or lyric, and Noeul estimates how much you'll understand before you start.

Watch words being learned

A saved word fades a step each time you read past it without looking it up; once it fades out, it moves to Learned. Flashcards (FSRS) catch the rare ones the story won't repeat.

Read anything on your screen

A floating OCR button reads text in any app — webtoons, subtitles, screenshots — and makes every word tappable, just like in the reader.

How it works

Three steps. Mostly just the first one.

  1. 1

    Open a book at your level

    Start with a built-in classic, import your own, or pick a song. Each one shows how much of it you'll understand — pick something a little easy. That's the point.

  2. 2

    Read; tap for instant help

    Hit an unknown word? Tap it. Definition, romanization or pinyin, root characters — then back to the story. Or just press play and listen along.

  3. 3

    Review only when you need to

    Most saved words fade out on their own as you keep meeting them. The rest wait in a quiet deck that surfaces them right before you'd forget — no sooner.

Under the hood

How Noeul learns your level.

No placement tests, no grades. Noeul keeps a quiet, on-device estimate of which words you know — and every part of the app draws on it.

Tell it where you're starting

On first launch, pick the language you're learning and your own. Then set your level: take a three-minute word check, pick it yourself from rows of the most common words (or an HSK level for Chinese), or start from scratch.

Reading sharpens it

Every word carries a difficulty grade, and every lookup, save, and flashcard review nudges your level estimate up or down. Together they give the odds you know any given word — before you ever tap it.

Every book gets a fit score

Import an EPUB or PDF and Noeul scans its vocabulary against your level: an estimate of how much you'll understand, shown before chapter one — and it keeps sharpening as you read.

Suggestions worth saving

A word is only suggested when Noeul is genuinely unsure you know it and it's too rare in the book for the story to teach it by repetition. Frequent words are left to reading — and new flashcards start on a schedule matched to how well you probably know them.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Is Noeul free?

Yes — Noeul is completely free. Download the APK above and install it on any Android 6.0+ phone; Android will ask you to allow installs from your browser, which is normal for apps outside the Play Store.

What level do I need to start?

If you can read hangul or Chinese characters and know a handful of words, you can begin. When you start, a three-minute word check sets your level — or pick it yourself, or start from scratch — and Noeul keeps refining its picture of your level as you read.

Does it work offline?

Partly. Definition lookups — including hanja and root characters — work offline, and focus mode works too. AI in-context meanings and translations need an internet connection, so those parts pause when you're offline.

Which languages can I learn?

Korean and Chinese today. Korean definitions are available in eleven languages, including English, Chinese, Spanish, French and Vietnamese. English learning is on the roadmap; if there's a language you think fits this read-first format, reach out and tell us.

Can I import my own books?

Yes. Noeul reads Korean and Chinese EPUB and PDF files, so you can bring your own books alongside the built-in public-domain classics. You can also add songs by searching for a title, artist or line of lyrics.
